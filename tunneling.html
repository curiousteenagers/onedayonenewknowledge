Quantum Tunneling

The strange quantum phenomenon that lets particles pass through seemingly impossible barriers.

Key Concepts

  • Superposition: A quantum state in which a particle can be described as a combination of multiple possible states at the same time, although the mathematical process of measurement is not explored.
  • Radioactive Alpha Decay: A type of radioactive decay that can be explained using quantum tunneling, though the nuclear forces involved are not discussed in detail.
  • Josephson Junction: A superconducting device that allows quantum effects to be controlled and is an important component of superconducting quantum computers.
  • Cooper Pairs: Pairs of electrons that form in superconducting materials at very low temperatures, allowing electrical current to flow with extremely low resistance.
  • Wave Function: A mathematical description of a quantum system that provides information about the probability of finding a particle in a particular state or location.

Big Question

"If particles can pass through barriers that appear impossible to cross, what does quantum tunneling reveal about the limits of what we consider physically possible?"

Questions to Explore

  • Could quantum tunneling eventually be used to create technologies that allow particles to move through barriers with unprecedented precision?
  • Could quantum computers use superposition and interference to solve certain problems that would be practically impossible for classical computers?
  • How would everyday technology change if superconducting processors could operate efficiently at room temperature?
