Tragedy of the Commons

Why shared resources are often overused despite everyone's best interests.

Key Concepts

  • Evolutionary biology: This is mentioned as Garrett Hardin's field of study, but the specific principles of how biological evolution interacts with human economic behavior are not explained [1].
  • Political economy: This is cited as the academic background of William Forster Lloyd without detailing the specific intersection of governance and resource distribution theory [2].
  • The Nobel Prize in Economics: This is referenced as the honor granted to Elinor Ostrom for her governance models, yet the selection criteria or the history of this prestigious award are not discussed.
  • Acid rain: This is identified as a direct consequence of discharging industrial waste into the air, though the chemical reactions between pollutants and moisture in the atmosphere are not explored.
  • Legal personhood: This is mentioned regarding the Whanganui River being granted human-like rights to protect its resources, but the jurisprudential mechanism for non-human entities is not detailed.

Vocabulary

Inevitability (C2) The state of being certain to happen and unable to be avoided or prevented.
Exploited (C2) Used unfairly for your own advantage or to help yourself.
Externalities (C2) Damage caused by a company's activities for which it does not pay, or something positive created by it for which it does not receive payment.
Incentive (C2) Something that encourages a person to do something or to work harder.
Collective (C1) Of or shared by every member of a group of people.

Big Question

"If rational self-interest leads to collective ruin, is the concept of a 'free market' inherently incompatible with the survival of a finite planet?".

Questions to Explore

  • What if we could give legal personhood to the entire Earth's atmosphere to allow the planet to sue polluters in a global court?
  • How would human social behavior change if our individual reputation was a digital currency that determined our daily access to shared public resources?
  • Could we design a "virtual commons" that teaches children the mathematical logic of sustainability through play before they ever enter the real economy?
