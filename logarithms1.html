Logarithms — Part 1: Properties, Operations, and Combined Calculations

Learn how to apply logarithmic properties, perform operations, and solve combined calculations step by step.

Key Concepts

  • Inverse of Exponentiation: Logarithms act as the mathematical inverse of exponentiation, designed specifically to extract a missing exponent from an exponential equation.
  • Core Algebraic Toolkit: Fundamental log rules simplify complex calculations by converting multiplication into addition, division into subtraction, and exponents into front coefficients.
  • Operational Adapter and Restrictions: The change of base formula converts logarithms for standard calculator processing, while strict rules prohibit non-positive arguments or bases equal to one.
  • Sequential Condensing Order: Simplifying complex logarithmic expressions requires a strict order of operations, starting with power rule coefficients before applying product and quotient rules.
  • Dodging Mathematical Illusion Traps: Common errors include falsely distributing logarithms over addition or confusing a quotient of two separate logarithms with the single fraction quotient rule.

Vocabulary

Inverse (C1) Opposite in position, direction, order, or effect, describing how logarithms reverse exponentiation to find missing exponents.
Coefficient (C2) A number or symbol multiplied with a variable, placed in front of a logarithmic term or moved to an exponent position.
Restriction (C1) A limiting condition or rule, such as requiring positive arguments and prohibiting bases equal to one.
Condense (C2) To make something more concise or compact, combining scattered logarithmic terms into one clean expression.
Illusion (C1) An idea or belief that is false or misleading, such as incorrectly distributing a logarithm across addition inside parentheses.
