The Doppler Effect

Why does an ambulance siren sound higher as it approaches and lower as it moves away? The Doppler Effect reveals how motion changes the frequency of waves we perceive.

Key Concepts

  • Wave Compression: When a moving source approaches an observer, the waves in front of it become closer together, producing a higher observed frequency.
  • Spectral Shifting: Light from an approaching astronomical object shifts toward shorter, bluer wavelengths, while light from a receding object shifts toward longer, redder wavelengths.
  • Constancy of Wave Speed: The speed of sound depends on the properties of the medium through which it travels, rather than directly on the speed of the source.
  • Relative Motion: The observed change in frequency depends on the relative motion between the source and the observer. Different observers can therefore measure different frequencies.
  • Radial Velocity Detection: Astronomers can detect planets by measuring tiny, periodic shifts in the spectrum of a star as the planet's gravity causes the star to move slightly toward and away from Earth.

Big Question

"If the frequency of a wave changes depending on the relative motion between its source and observer, how much of what we perceive about the universe depends on our own position within it?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would astronomy change if scientists could no longer assume that observed shifts in starlight were caused by relative motion?
  • Could increasingly precise measurements of stellar motion allow astronomers to discover smaller and more distant planets?
  • If two observers moving differently relative to the same source measure different frequencies, which measurement should be considered the "true" frequency?
