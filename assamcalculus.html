Assembly Calculus

Assembly Calculus proposes that groups of strongly connected neurons can combine, associate, and manipulate information, providing a possible framework for understanding how symbolic thought emerges from neural activity.

Key Concepts

  • The Missing Neural Operating System: Neuroscience still faces a major challenge in explaining how patterns of activity across individual neurons give rise to complex cognitive functions such as language, memory, and reasoning.
  • Neural Assembly Mechanics: Assembly Calculus proposes that cognition is not encoded by isolated "grandmother cells," but by coordinated groups of strongly interconnected excitatory neurons called neural assemblies.
  • Self-Organizing Forces: Neural assemblies can emerge through interactions between spontaneous neural activity, Hebbian plasticity, and mechanisms that limit excessive activation, allowing stable patterns of representation to develop.
  • The Assembly Calculus Operations: Developed by Christos Papadimitriou and collaborators, the framework describes mathematical operations such as project, associate, and merge that can manipulate and combine neural representations.
  • Symbolic Thought and Syntax: Assembly Calculus proposes that combinations of neural assemblies could represent relationships between concepts and support hierarchical structures similar to those used in language and symbolic reasoning.

Big Question

"If complex thought emerges from mathematical operations performed by self-organizing neural assemblies, could Assembly Calculus eventually provide a unified framework for explaining how biological neural activity becomes symbolic thought?"

Questions to Explore

  • How might future neuroimaging technologies overcome the limitations of fMRI and EEG to observe large populations of individual neurons simultaneously in living human brains?
  • Could artificial neural networks use operations such as project, associate, and merge to improve symbolic reasoning and syntactic language processing?
  • What if disruptions to the mechanisms that create and maintain neural assemblies contribute to certain forms of memory impairment?
