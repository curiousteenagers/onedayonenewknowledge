Why Do Bank Runs Happen?

Discover how fear and a lack of confidence can cause large numbers of people to withdraw their money from a bank at the same time.

Key Concepts

  • Fractional reserve system: Banks keep only a tiny portion of deposited funds on hand for daily transactions while lending out the rest, which helps drive the economy but requires extremely careful oversight.
  • The Diamond-Dybvig model: This award-winning framework explains why even fundamentally sound financial institutions are vulnerable to sudden panics when depositors act on rumors.
  • The fear loop: This self-fulfilling cycle occurs when a rumor of insolvency forces banks into rapid fire sales of long-term assets at a loss, creating the very bankruptcy depositors feared.
  • Silent bank runs: In the digital era, panic moves at the speed of the internet as depositors use online transfers to move vast sums instantly without queuing outside.
  • Lender of last resort: Central banks can step in to provide immediate cash to solvent but illiquid institutions, stopping financial contagion from spreading.

Big Question

"If the stability of our entire global financial network relies entirely on a shared psychological illusion of confidence, does this mean our modern wealth is only as real as our collective willingness to believe in it?"

Questions to Explore

  • What if digital banking platforms were designed to intentionally slow down transaction speeds during periods of market stress to prevent lightning-fast panic?
  • Could deposit insurance limits be dynamically adjusted in real time based on a depositor's level of financial sophistication to maintain fairness in the system?
  • If central banks can always print money to act as a lender of last resort, why do we still permit the existence of fractional reserve systems that are inherently vulnerable to collapse?
