The Double Slit Experiment

Why time flows differently at extreme speeds and in strong gravity.

Key Concepts

  • Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle: This is cited as the reason why any measurement, no matter how delicate, inevitably destroys the interference pattern, but the specific mathematical relationship between position and momentum is not explained [1].
  • Quantum Entanglement: This phenomenon is identified as the mechanism interacting with the inherent fuzziness of atoms to erase wavelike properties, yet the process of how these particles become linked is not discussed [2].
  • Daltons: This unit is used to describe the massive weight of bulky molecules fired through the slits, but its formal definition or relationship to other units of mass is not provided [3].
  • The Vacuum: This is mentioned as the specific environment required for the MIT researchers to float single atoms, though the technical details of how such a void is maintained are not explored [1].
  • Primordial Black Holes: These are briefly referenced as potential formations from the start of the universe that might act as dark matter, but their physical differences from standard black holes are not explained [2].

Vocabulary

Big Question

"If the physical act of measurement forces the universe to pick a definitive state, does reality exist in a coherent form when it is not being observed or interacting with anything else?" [2, 6, 7].

Questions to Explore

  • What would happen to our perception of the world if we could perceive the "wave of probabilities" of every object before it was measured? [5, 7].
  • If the quantum eraser experiment suggests that the present can rewrite the past, could we eventually use measurement to manipulate historical outcomes? [2].
  • How would human technology change if we could master "superposition" to perform tasks in multiple locations at the exact same time? [5].
