The Circular Economy

A sustainable economic model that reduces waste by reusing resources.

Key Concepts

  • Biomass: This is mentioned as one of the major categories of materials humanity consumes every year, but its specific biological or chemical components are not explained [1].
  • Greenhouse gas emissions: These are cited as a primary driver of the planetary crisis and a byproduct of the linear process, though the specific gas types or their atmospheric interactions are not detailed [2], [3].
  • Warranty: This is referenced in the context of planned obsolescence and devices failing shortly after they expire, but the legal framework of these agreements is not discussed [4], [5].
  • Renewable energy: This is identified as a transition just as vital for the future as the circular model, yet the specific technologies or power sources are not explored [3].
  • Carbon emissions: These are used to quantify the potential success of circular strategies, but the physical nature of how they are released or stored is not explained [3].

Vocabulary

Fundamental (C1) Forming the base, from which everything else develops [1], [2].
Discard (C1) To throw something away because it is no longer wanted or needed [1], [4].
Notorious (C1) Famous for something immoral or bad [6].
Robust (C1) (Of a person or animal) strong and healthy, or (of an object or system) strong and unlikely to break or fail [5].
Comprehensive (C1) Complete and including everything that is necessary [3].

Big Question

"If the byproduct of one process must become the nutrient for another, can a truly circular society exist within a culture that values individual ownership over collective utility?" [7], [6], [8].

Questions to Explore

  • How would our cities look if every neighborhood had a shared library of things where we used assets instead of owning them? [9], [6].
  • Could we use artificial intelligence to predict exactly when a modular part will fail so that repairs happen before a device stops working? [7], [5].
  • What if the concept of garbage was removed from our vocabulary and replaced with the term "valuable feedstock"? [7].
