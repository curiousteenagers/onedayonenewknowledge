Cognitive Dissonance

The mental discomfort of holding conflicting beliefs or behaviors.

Key Concepts

  • Public Policy: Cited as a realm where personal allegiances often dictate thinking, but the structural processes of governing or creating these policies are not explained.
  • Virus Screening: Mentioned as a life-saving medical procedure that causes anxiety, though the actual biological or diagnostic steps of such tests are not described.
  • Fuel Economy: Used as a specific factor people focus on to justify a car purchase, but the technical engineering behind fuel efficiency is not explored.
  • Avant-Garde Masterpiece: A term used to describe how someone might rebrand a terrible experience to resolve regret, but the history or definition of avant-garde art movements is not provided.
  • Blind Spots: Identified as the psychological space where contradictions hide, yet the neurological mechanisms behind why we fail to perceive our own biases are not detailed.

Vocabulary

Allegiance (C2) Loyalty and support for a political party, religion, ruler, or ideology.
Erode (C2) To slowly reduce or destroy something.
Gratification (C1) A feeling of pleasure and satisfaction.
Malicious (C1) Intended to harm or upset other people.
Indisputable (C2) True, and impossible to doubt.

Big Question

"If our minds are hardwired to rewrite reality simply to avoid discomfort, can we ever truly trust our own memories of being a 'good' person?"

Questions to Explore

  • What if smart devices could send us a notification every time they detect us using a mental shortcut to avoid a difficult truth?
  • Could we design an education system where "changing one's mind" is rewarded with more social prestige than being consistently right?
  • How would human interaction change if we could physically feel the "fire alarm" of someone else's internal inconsistency as if it were our own?
