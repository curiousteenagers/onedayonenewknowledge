Utilitarianism

How should we decide what is morally right when our choices affect many people? Explore Utilitarianism, an ethical theory associated with Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill, which evaluates actions according to their consequences and their capacity to promote overall well-being.

Key Concepts

  • Plato's Dual Universes: Plato divided reality into a sensory physical world of changing shadows and an eternal intelligible realm of pure forms accessible only through rational soul purification.
  • The Supreme Idea of the Good: Positioned as the cosmic sun illuminating all truth, the Good acts as a real, intelligible, foundational, and realizable principle guiding both personal ethics and political governance.
  • Aristotle's Third Man Critique: Aristotle challenged Platonic separation by arguing that isolated forms cannot cause physical movement, demonstrating that infinite logical regress collapses disconnected metaphysics into mere homonymy.
  • Immanent Forms and Biological Reproduction: Re-anchoring forms directly within physical matter, Aristotle defined nature as an internal principle of motion where individual organisms achieve eternity through continuous reproduction.
  • Neo-Kantian Methodological Reinterpretation: Twentieth-century thinker Paul Natorp reinterpreted the Good not as a glowing transcendent entity, but as the foundational rule of logical law that unifies scientific rationality.

Vocabulary

Intelligible (C2) Capable of being comprehended by human reason rather than perceived through sensory observation.
Immanent (C2) Present and operating within physical matter and natural processes rather than existing in a separate realm.
Regress (C2) A continuous returning or backward chain of logical steps, such as an infinite series of explanatory forms.
Homonymy (C2) The state where two entities share an identical name while possessing completely different definitions or natures.
Bedrock (C2) The solid fundamental principle or foundation upon which a philosophical system or method of reasoning rests.

Big Question

"If the fundamental architecture of reality is defined either by transcendent eternal forms, immanent biological principles, or logical rules of thought, can human reason ever observe physical existence without an underlying conceptual framework?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would modern scientific inquiry change if researchers treated natural laws as immanent biological blueprints rather than abstract mathematical abstractions?
  • Could artificial intelligence systems develop philosophical reasoning if their cognitive models relied on Natorp's rule of logical law to structure incoming data?
  • What if neuroimaging could identify whether human decision-making aligns with Plato's tripartite division of the soul between basic appetite, spirit, and reason?
