Biology

Evolution

Discover how natural selection, genetic variation, and environmental pressures drive the evolution of species.

Key Concepts

  • Lamarckism: Lamarck proposed that characteristics acquired during an organism's lifetime could be inherited by its offspring. Modern genetics showed that this is not how inheritance generally works.
  • Natural Selection: Evolution by natural selection occurs when heritable variations affect survival or reproduction, causing advantageous traits to become more common over generations.
  • Galápagos Finches: Darwin's observations of finches with different beak shapes provided an important example of how populations can diversify as they adapt to different environments and food sources.
  • Industrial Melanism: During the Industrial Revolution, darker peppered moths became more common in some polluted areas because their coloration provided better camouflage against soot-darkened surfaces.
  • Evidence for Evolution: Evolution is supported by multiple independent lines of evidence, including fossils, genetics, comparative anatomy, biogeography, and direct observations of populations changing over time.

Vocabulary

Evolve (C1) To develop gradually over time into a different or more advanced form.
Modify (C1) To change something slightly in order to improve it or make it more suitable.
Ancestor (C1) An organism from the past from which later organisms have evolved.
Advantageous (C2) Providing a benefit or increasing the likelihood of success.
Mutate (C2) To undergo a change in genetic material that can produce a new variation.

Big Question

"If natural selection can gradually transform populations through inherited variation, how does this change the way we understand the remarkable diversity of life on Earth?"

Questions to Explore

  • How might climate change alter the traits that natural selection favors in plants and animals living in rapidly changing environments?
  • Could modern human environments create new evolutionary pressures that cause certain genetic traits to become more common over many generations?
  • How could new fossil discoveries or genetic evidence change our understanding of the evolutionary history of a particular species?
