Game Theory of Everyday Life

How strategic thinking shapes the choices we make every day.

Key Concepts

  • Bounded Rationality: Mentioned as a psychological constraint where limited mental energy and lack of perfect information prevent us from making perfect choices, but the specific cognitive theories behind these limits are not explained.
  • Free Rider: Cited in the context of group projects where one person might benefit from the work of others, though the formal economic principles governing this social behavior are not discussed.
  • Zero-Sum Conflict: Identified as a type of high-stakes situation where one person's gain is exactly equal to another's loss, but the mathematical properties of these interactions remain unexplored.
  • Bargaining Position: Mentioned as a critical factor in salary negotiations that involves controlling information flow, yet the specific tactics to strengthen or evaluate this position are not detailed.
  • Collective Rationality: Described as the ideal outcome for a group that often conflicts with individual self-interest, though the broader philosophical frameworks for this logic are not provided.

Vocabulary

Anticipate (Level C-One) To imagine or expect that something will happen.
Aggressive (Level C-One) Using or showing force or pressure in order to succeed.
Optimal (Level C-Two) The best or most effective possible in a particular situation.
Altruism (Level C-Two) Willingness to help others even if it results in disadvantage for yourself.
Haggle (Level C-Two) To attempt to decide on a price or conditions that are acceptable to the person selling and the person buying by arguing.

Big Question

"If the mathematical gravity of the Nash Equilibrium pulls us toward outcomes that harm the collective, is human progress defined by our ability to defy our own rational self-interest?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would the global economy change if transparency were absolute, making it impossible to hide your bargaining position during a deal?
  • Could we design a social credit system where the "payout" for altruism is mathematically higher than the reward for being a free rider?
  • What if we could use computer modeling to solve everyday household chores so that no one ever has to play a game of chicken with the trash again?
