The Tolley Problem

The famous ethical dilemma that challenges our moral instincts.

Key Concepts

  • Railway Switch: The track mechanism that allows a train to change direction, forming the basis of the classic Trolley Problem, though its mechanical operation is not explained.
  • Footbridge: The setting for a variation of the Trolley Problem in which a person can stop the trolley through direct physical intervention, without discussing the structure of the bridge itself.
  • Electric Shock: Used in experiments to simulate harmful consequences and study moral decision-making, though the science behind electricity is not explored.
  • Life-Saving Ventilator: A medical device referenced in discussions of resource allocation during health crises, without explaining how it supports breathing.
  • Lines of Code: The programming instructions that translate ethical rules into the behavior of autonomous systems, though specific programming languages are not discussed.

Vocabulary

Agonizing (C1) Causing extreme physical or emotional pain.

Big Question

"If our moral decisions are shaped more by emotion than logic, should we expect artificial intelligence to make more ethical choices than humans?"

Questions to Explore

  • Who should be held responsible when an autonomous vehicle makes a life-or-death decision—the programmer, the manufacturer, or the machine itself?
  • Do our choices under pressure reveal our true moral values better than the answers we give in calm philosophical discussions?
  • Should an artificial intelligence ever be allowed to sacrifice one person to save many others, or should some moral boundaries never be crossed?
