Economics

Monetarism

Discover how the amount of money circulating in an economy can influence inflation, prices, and economic stability—and why monetarist economists place such importance on controlling the money supply.

Key Concepts

  • The Quantity Equation: This economic model expresses the relationship between the money supply, the velocity of money, the price level, and real economic output.
  • The Constant Growth Rule: This policy proposal suggests that the money supply should grow at a steady and predictable rate rather than being adjusted frequently in response to economic conditions.
  • The Great Contraction: This term refers to the severe decline in the money supply and economic activity during the early years of the Great Depression, which monetarist economists argue significantly worsened the crisis.
  • Long and Variable Lags: This principle describes how changes in monetary policy can take a considerable and unpredictable amount of time to affect the wider economy.
  • The New Neoclassical Synthesis: This modern economic framework combines ideas from different schools of macroeconomics, including monetary policy, expectations, market behavior, and Keynesian concepts of economic stabilization.

Big Question

"If changes in the money supply can strongly influence inflation and economic activity, how much power should central banks have over the economy, and can their decisions ever be completely predictable?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would the global economy change if central banks followed a completely automated rule for controlling the money supply?
  • If financial innovations make the velocity of money increasingly difficult to predict, how can economists accurately measure the relationship between money and economic activity?
  • Could a different monetary policy during the Great Depression have significantly reduced the severity and duration of the economic crisis?
