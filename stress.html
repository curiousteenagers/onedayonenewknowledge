Sleep, Stress, and the Science of Decision-Making

Discover how sleep and stress can influence attention, memory, emotions, and the way we make everyday decisions.

Key Concepts

  • The Rationality Myth: Classical economics assumed humans evaluate choices using stable logic, but neuroeconomics demonstrates that our decisions are dynamically manipulated by transient physical states like hunger and fatigue.
  • The Ghrelin Impulsivity Cascade: When the stomach is empty, it secretes ghrelin, which travels via the vagus nerve to the brain's reward centers, spiking dopamine and driving impulsive choices even in non-food financial scenarios.
  • Decoupling Executive Control: Sleep deprivation causes a metabolic drop in the prefrontal cortex, severing its functional connection to the emotional threat detection center in the amygdala.
  • Model-Free Regression: As fatigue dismantles vigilant attention over hours of wakefulness, the brain shifts from forward-thinking model-based planning to intuitive model-free habits that ignore warning signs.
  • Contextual Biological Resistance: Biological impulses do not dictate strict destiny, as individual physiological baselines—such as ghrelin resistance observed in specific medical conditions—can modify behavioral outcomes.

Big Question

"How much of a decision is determined by what we think and how much by the biological state of the brain making a choice?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would corporate decision-making structures change if high-stakes financial choices were required to be made only after verifying that executives are well-rested and nourished?
  • Could we develop targeted neurofeedback therapies that help individuals maintain functional connectivity between the prefrontal cortex and amygdala during acute sleep deprivation?
  • How might our legal and ethical frameworks regarding personal responsibility evolve if neuroeconomics proves that severe metabolic deficits functionally override logical choice?
