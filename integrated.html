The Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness

A theory that proposes consciousness arises from the way information is integrated within the brain.

Key Concepts

  • Intrinsic Existence: The idea that conscious experience exists from its own subjective perspective, although the physical basis of this property remains debated.
  • Phi: A quantity used in Integrated Information Theory to represent the amount of integrated information generated by a system.
  • Shannon Information: A mathematical measure of information associated with the reduction of uncertainty, commonly used in information theory and communication systems.
  • Perturbational Complexity Index: A method that combines brain stimulation with measurements of neural activity to help assess levels of consciousness.
  • The Unfolding Argument: A mathematical argument suggesting that certain feed-forward systems can reproduce the same input-output behavior as recurrent systems while differing in their proposed level of integrated information.

Big Question

"If consciousness depends on the way information is integrated within a physical system, could subjective experience eventually be explained through mathematics alone?"

Questions to Explore

  • If an artificial system developed an extremely high level of integrated information, what evidence would we need before considering it potentially conscious?
  • Could the Perturbational Complexity Index improve our ability to distinguish different states of consciousness in patients with severe brain injuries?
  • Could principles from Integrated Information Theory help researchers design artificial systems that process information more efficiently?
