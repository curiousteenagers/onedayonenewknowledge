Pythagorean Theorem

Discover how one of mathematics' most famous equations connects the three sides of every right-angled triangle—and how the mysterious irrationality of √2 challenged ancient ideas about numbers.

Key Concepts

  • The 3-4-5 Rule: This practical application of the Pythagorean theorem uses side lengths of 3, 4, and 5 units to create a precise right angle.
  • The Hypotenuse: The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right-angled triangle and is always opposite the ninety-degree angle.
  • Ancient Babylonian Origins: Mathematical evidence shows that Babylonians understood relationships between the sides of right triangles long before the theorem was associated with Pythagoras.
  • Working Backwards: The theorem can be rearranged to calculate an unknown side when the hypotenuse and one other side are known.
  • The Horizon Calculation: The Pythagorean theorem can be used with the Earth's radius to estimate how far away the horizon is from an observer at a given height.

Big Question

"If a mathematical relationship understood thousands of years ago can describe everything from a building corner to the geometry of Earth's horizon, what does this reveal about the role of mathematics in describing the physical world?"
