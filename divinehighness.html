The Problem of Divine Hiddenness

If God is perfectly loving, why do sincere people fail to believe in Him? Explore Schellenberg’s argument and its main objections.

Key Concepts

  • Existential Lament to Logical Proof: The visceral human experience of divine silence evolved in the early nineteen nineties from a personal crisis into a formal logical argument against the existence of an all-loving deity.
  • Schellenberg's Hiddenness Argument: Philosopher John Schellenberg posits that an unsurpassably loving God would inherently value personal relationships and never permit non-resistant non-belief, making the existence of earnest seekers proof that such a deity does not exist.
  • The Involuntary Nature of Belief: Forming a belief requires sufficient grounding evidence, meaning that without basic manifestations of divine presence, honest seekers cannot rationally form the initial belief necessary to initiate a relationship.
  • Theistic Counterarguments and Transcendent Distinctions: Defenders of theism counter that an infinite creator cannot be discovered as a physical object inside creation—much like looking for Shakespeare within Hamlet—and that universal moral law already provides intrinsic evidence.
  • Eschatological Delay and Free Will: Philosophical theology suggests that overt, undeniable divine manifestations would undermine human free will and force compliance, whereas temporary silence preserves authentic moral growth and voluntary love.

Vocabulary

Involuntary (C1) Done without conscious control or intention, describing how forming a belief depends strictly on compelling evidence rather than simple choice.
Transcendent (C2) Existing beyond or independent of the physical material universe, reflecting how an infinite creator cannot be detected through ordinary empirical tools.
Incompatible (C2) Incapable of existing together in harmony, characterizing the logical argument's claim that divine love cannot coexist with human doubt.
Eschatological (C2) Relating to the final destiny of the soul and human history, describing the idea that ultimate divine revelation is delayed until the end of time.
Epistemic (C2) Relating to knowledge or the conditions of validation, describing how human flaws or moral corruption might block spiritual perception.

Big Question

"If an all-loving deity desires a personal relationship with humanity, does the existence of open-minded seekers who find no evidence of divine presence logically disprove God, or does divine hiddenness preserve genuine human autonomy?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would modern religious philosophy adapt if neuroscientific tools could objectively determine whether an individual is genuinely non-resistant rather than subconsciously suppressing evidence?
  • Could public moral consciousness act as a sufficient epistemic anchor for spiritual belief without requiring physical or miraculous divine intervention?
  • What if global communication networks provided undeniable celestial proof of a higher power tomorrow; would that foster authentic human love or merely compel forced obedience?
