Economics

Capitalism

The economic system that transformed how people produce, trade, and build wealth.

Key Concepts

  • Wall Street Stock Tickers: Traditional symbols of financial activity that represent the broader reach and influence of modern capitalism.
  • Feudal System: A medieval socio-economic structure based on land ownership, social hierarchy, and obligations between different classes.
  • Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation: A U.S. government agency created during the New Deal to protect bank deposits and promote stability in the banking system.
  • Social Security: A government program designed to provide financial support to eligible citizens, particularly during retirement or periods of economic hardship.
  • Tariffs: Taxes placed on imported goods that can influence international trade, domestic industries, and consumer prices.

Vocabulary

Monopoly (C2) Complete control of a particular market or area of business by one company or organization.
Exploitation (C2) The unfair use of a person, situation, or resource to gain an advantage.
Pursuit (C1) The act of trying to achieve, obtain, or find something.
Inequality (C1) An unfair difference in wealth, opportunities, rights, or resources between people or groups.
Undeniable (C1) Clearly true and impossible to reasonably doubt.

Big Question

"If capitalism is deeply embedded in modern society, can we reduce its negative effects while preserving the innovation and economic freedom it can create?"

Questions to Explore

  • What would happen if companies were rewarded not only for profits but also for improving the economic stability of their local communities?
  • Could technology help governments predict when markets are becoming dangerously concentrated before a monopoly develops?
  • How would society change if national progress were measured by quality of life and economic equality rather than economic growth alone?
