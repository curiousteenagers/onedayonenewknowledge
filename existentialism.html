Existentialism

What gives life meaning when there is no predetermined answer? Explore how existentialist philosophers confronted freedom, responsibility, meaning, anxiety, and the challenge of creating a life that feels authentic.

Key Concepts

  • The Collapse of Objective Meaning: Friedrich Nietzsche warned that the collapse of divine authority shattered traditional morality, forcing individuals to face nihilism and forge their own values from scratch.
  • Existence Precedes Essence: Jean-Paul Sartre asserted that humans lack a pre-programmed nature, distinguishing undetermined human consciousness from static physical objects whose purposes are defined before creation.
  • The Weight of Absolute Freedom: Søren Kierkegaard described existential dread as the dizziness of freedom, while Sartre noted we are condemned to be free because no external rules relieve our total personal responsibility.
  • Facticity and Creative Transcendence: While unchosen physical constraints like birthplace and biology limit our starting conditions, true autonomy lies in how we interpret and actively respond to those circumstances.
  • The Trap of Bad Faith: Hiding from freedom by acting like a mechanical automaton or pretending to possess a fixed identity is a self-deceptive attempt to escape existential anxiety.

Vocabulary

Radical (C2) Relating to the most important, basic, or essential parts of something, describing our complete freedom to choose who we become.
Condemn (C2) To force someone to experience something difficult or unpleasant, such as being bound to absolute freedom without a script.
Ambiguity (C2) The quality of being open to more than one interpretation or lacking clear boundaries, which authentic individuals must embrace.
Oppression (C2) Cruel or unjust exercise of authority, which Simone de Beauvoir argued authentic individuals must actively resist to expand human freedom.
Rebel (C1) To fight against authority or refuse to accept established rules, creating profound personal meaning in an indifferent world.

Big Question

"If we are condemned to absolute freedom without a pre-written script, is the anxiety of existence a burdensome curse or the ultimate catalyst for human liberation?"

Questions to Explore

  • How would educational systems transform if schools taught students to view existential anxiety as a healthy teacher of personal responsibility rather than a biological defect?
  • Could corporate environments eliminate bad faith by encouraging employees to express authentic creativity rather than performing rigid, mechanical roles?
  • What if social movements utilized Simone de Beauvoir's framework to demonstrate that individual freedom is impossible without actively liberating marginalized communities?
